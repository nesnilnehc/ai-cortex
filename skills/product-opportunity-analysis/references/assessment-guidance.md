# Assessing conflicting or limited opportunity evidence

## Compare scope and source independence

- Recheck the report schema, freshness, scope and provenance for material claims. Compare product version and edition, target users, jurisdiction, period and market definition before treating two findings as comparable.
- Keep findings from different scopes distinct. Qualify the claim to the supported scope or record an Unknown for the target scope. When comparable sources disagree, record both findings, cite the strongest opposing evidence and name what would resolve the conflict.
- Count related sources as one provenance family. A vendor announcement and coverage that repeats it support attribution to that vendor. Independent customer interviews, usage or feedback provide a separate basis for a user-demand claim.

## Calibrate estimates and confidence

- For size or impact, identify the eligible population, compatible unit and period, method, assumptions and claim IDs supporting the inputs. Use null when those inputs or the method are missing.
- Match confidence to lane completeness, source independence and agreement on claims that could change the decision. Record the assumption most likely to overturn the recommendation and a validation step with an observable outcome.

## Examples

- Two dated reports identify an unmet workflow but no authorized user evidence exists. Record the gap, cite supporting and opposing findings, and recommend exploration with a validation step tied to demand.
- One report covers a paid enterprise edition and another covers a free edition elsewhere. Preserve each finding's scope and record an Unknown if neither establishes the decisive claim for the target market.
